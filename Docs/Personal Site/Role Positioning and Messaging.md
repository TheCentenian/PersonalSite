## How to position yourself (language to use on the site)

- **Do not:** “Frontend developer” or “I code in React.”
- **Do:** **Engineer focused on blockchain usability and transaction clarity** — and/or **product architect** / **systems thinker** who builds in the Sui ecosystem.

**One-line positioning (e.g. About or Home):**  
*“I design and build systems that make blockchain usable—transaction clarity, digital asset ownership, event-driven apps—and I’ve been shipping prototypes and platform infrastructure on Sui for the past year and a half.”*

**Evarra Tracker (transaction readability):**  
*“Built an application that connects to Sui wallets, signs transactions, and translates raw blockchain activity into human-readable reports to improve transparency and usability for non-technical users.”*  
→ This is the kind of problem wallet and product teams care about.

**Overall narrative:**  
You work in four overlapping areas: (1) full-stack application development, (2) blockchain application integration (wallet, signing, on-chain verification), (3) smart-contract-level systems (Move modules for scores, achievements, badges, tournaments), (4) product/systems thinking (economies, rewards, progression). That hybrid profile is rare and valuable to Web3 product teams, game teams, and infrastructure teams.

## What hiring managers care about (proof of work)

- **Shipped work:** MVPs and prototypes that work, not just ideas.
- **Architecture:** Modular design, clear boundaries, “design for stability and long-term investment.”
- **Blockchain integration:** Wallet connection, signing flows, transaction interpretation, on-chain verification.
- **Your role:** What you *designed* and *implemented* (not “worked on” vaguely).
- **Sui specifically:** Object model, capabilities, transaction blocks, digital assets — you’ve built with these.

So on the site: for each project, make it easy to see **problem → architecture → your responsibility → key technical signals** (e.g. wallet integration, Move modules, event-driven systems). Links to GitHub, demos, or screenshots strengthen the signal.
