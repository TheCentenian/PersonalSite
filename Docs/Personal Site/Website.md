## Privacy & Identity (Doxxing Concern)

How do I balance privacy though? I don't like having my picture and name out there. I don't want to be "doxxed".

That concern is very common in Web3, and you actually **do not need to fully dox yourself to get hired**. Many respected builders maintain a **semi public identity**.

The key is to separate **public professional identity** from **personal identity**.

You want to be **credible but not over exposed**.

---

### First: What You Do NOT Need

You do not need to publish:

- your home location  
- your personal photo  
- your family  
- personal social media  
- detailed personal history  

Many Web3 developers never share these publicly.

---

### Second: What You SHOULD Share

You do need a **professional identity anchor** so teams know you are a real person.

This can be minimal:

- Name  
- Email  
- GitHub  
- Portfolio  
- X account (optional)  

That is enough.

You can even list your location broadly like: United States / North America / Remote

---

### Third: Photo Is Optional

Photos are not required. Many Web3 engineers **do not use profile photos** on their portfolio sites.

Alternatives that work well:

- simple logo  
- geometric avatar  
- abstract symbol  
- stylized illustration  

This keeps identity consistent without exposing your real appearance.

---

### Fourth: Pseudonym Strategy (Common in Web3)

You can operate publicly as:

- **Luis Centeno** (professional identity)  
- or **Visioneer / SuiTwo** (project identity)  

Many builders do this: Public brand / Real identity shared privately during hiring.

Employers care more about: skill, collaboration, code quality. They rarely care about public doxxing.

---

### Fifth: What Happens During Hiring

Eventually you will provide real information when:

- signing employment contracts  
- background checks  
- payroll setup  

But that happens **privately**, not on your website.

---

### Sixth: Portfolio Privacy Strategy

A good balance looks like this.

**Public site shows:**

- your work  
- your thinking  
- your GitHub  
- contact email  

Private details shared only when necessary.
